\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Scenario.} A synthetic IT service-desk desktop workflow with four hidden roles (L1 agent, L2 senior, L3 specialist, supervisor) that differ in which triage / diagnosis blocks run in parallel, which steps are skipped, and which gated UI actions (\texttt{escalate}, \texttt{approve}, \texttt{force\_close}) are on screen; enabled activities come from screenshots, so recordings carry noise.
\item \textbf{$\kappa$ at zero noise.} $\kappa$ recovers all four roles exactly (ARI 1.00, 4 views) with no cluster count, distance, or threshold; each induced identifier is one role (the supervisor view exposes \texttt{approve} / \texttt{force\_close}, absent from the agent view).
\item \textbf{$\kappa$ under noise.} Its ARI falls to 0.56 at 5\%, 0.30 at 10\%, 0.13 at 15\%, 0.06 at 20\%, 0.01 at 30\%, 0.00 at 40\%; its view count rises from 4 to 954 --- each corrupted trace becomes its own view.
\item \textbf{Crossover.} The control-flow clustering baseline (mean of five, $k=\mathrm{GT}$) overtakes $\kappa$ on ARI at the first non-zero noise step ($\approx$0.05); it is otherwise flat (ARI 0.66--0.74 across all levels) and never recovers the roles.
\item \textbf{Recorded role attribute.} Clustering on the \emph{clean} case attributes (which include \texttt{role}) stays at ARI 0.65--0.91 at every noise level --- the role label survives enabled-set noise, but the analyst must have recorded it and must supply $k$. $\kappa$ needs neither.
\item \textbf{Enabled-only clustering.} $k$-means on enabled-activity features is erratic under noise (ARI 0.57--0.81) and does not track $\kappa$.
\item \textbf{Variant grouping.} Classical- and translucent-variant grouping reach ARI $\le$0.27 even at zero noise (they over-separate into 93+ groups) and are unaffected by noise.
\item \textbf{Global model.} One model over the whole log yields a single group (ARI 0) at every noise level.
\item \textbf{Fitness.} Every approach's per-group models replay their own sub-logs exactly (fitness $=1.00$ throughout), so the classic axes reduce to precision / F1.
\item \textbf{Translucent fitness.} At zero noise $\kappa$'s per-view models reach translucent fitness 1.00 (translucent F1 1.00), the best of all approaches; the single global model is the weakest (0.61).
\item \textbf{Model quality vs.\ noise.} As noise rises $\kappa$'s translucent fitness falls to nan (its views are now noise fragments); the clustering baselines' model quality is roughly flat because their partitions barely change.
\item \textbf{Generalisation.} Variant grouping generalises worst ($\approx$0.76) --- one trace per model; $\kappa$ and the clustering baselines are $\approx0.9$.
\item \textbf{Precision / F1.} With fitness pinned at $1$, F1 tracks precision. At zero noise $\kappa$, variant grouping and every $k=\mathrm{GT}$ clustering baseline sit at precision 1.00; as noise rises $\kappa$ holds 0.89--0.96 while the control-flow baselines fall to nan (their sub-logs now mix roles).
\item \textbf{Takeaway.} When the enabled sets are trustworthy $\kappa$ recovers the roles exactly and parameter-free and its per-view models are the most faithful to the enabled behaviour; once the screenshots are noisy $\kappa$ over-segments and loses that edge, while the other approaches --- noise-insensitive but never accurate --- still do not recover the roles. Robustness to enabled-set noise is the open problem.
\end{itemize}
